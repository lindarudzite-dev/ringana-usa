where it started…

It started with morning sickness and a very long ingredient list

In January 2023, I was six months into being someone's mom — and about two years into becoming, without really meaning to, an ingredient detective. My first pregnancy came with morning sickness so intense that ordinary smells became unbearable, including, it turned out, the synthetic fragrance in almost everything I owned. So I started reading labels. Really reading them.

I wanted our home as toxin-free as I could get it before my baby arrived — no compromise, no "good enough." That's how I found Ringana: fresh, honestly-made products with nothing to hide on the label. I fell in love with them long before I ever thought about the business side.

why I said yes…

Motherhood didn't shrink my ambition — it just needed a new shape

Once I discovered the products, the business opportunity found me. And it made sense in a way that surprised me: I loved what I was already using, I believed in the values behind it, and here was a real way to build some income, set my own goals, and find a community of women who got it — without spending every waking hour just cooking and changing diapers.

It became my way of finding myself again inside motherhood — not instead of it.

a first of its kind…

I was the first to build this in Latvia

My mentor is based in Germany — there was no team, no playbook, and no one else doing this in my country when I started. I became the first person to build a Ringana team in Latvia, learning as I went and bringing along every woman curious enough to ask what I was using, drinking, or glowing from.

Today, that's grown into a like-minded team of women across the Baltics — not a huge machine, just people who wanted the same thing I did: something of their own.

the next first…

Ringana is opening in America — and someone will be first there, too

Ringana was founded in Austria in 1996 by Andreas Wilfinger and Ulla Wannemacher, after they read the ingredient list on their son's toothpaste and didn't like what they found. Nearly three decades later, that same family still leads the company — and in 2026, Ringana is investing $85 million in its first U.S. headquarters and production facility in Roanoke, Virginia.

I know exactly what it feels like to be the first person building this somewhere new — because I was, in Latvia. The U.S. market hasn't opened yet. That means there's still a "first" to become, in your town, your state, your circle of friends.

what moms ask me…

Questions I hear all the time

I don't have time for another job.

Neither did I. This isn't a second job with a schedule — it's minutes stacked into a business: a message sent during nap time, an order placed after bedtime. It grows in the cracks of your day, not instead of them.

I'm not a "salesy" person.

Good — neither am I. What actually works is sharing products you already love with people who ask. The community and the products do most of the talking.

Isn't this just another MLM?

I get asked this a lot, and I'd rather you ask than wonder. What drew me in was the product first — I was a customer for months before I ever considered the business. There's no pressure to recruit or stock inventory you don't need; it's built around real products people actually reorder.
